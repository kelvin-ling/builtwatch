# October 20: AI impact briefs and an attention-first overview

## AI that earns its place

Rules matching stays the detector: deterministic, free, quiet on repeats. It answers
"this source mentions a dependency you recorded", not "does this matter to my app". An
**AI impact brief** now answers that for each new rules review, in one bounded call:

- **Input:** the review's quoted source passage (as untrusted, delimited data), the app's
  profile and its backend map. **Output (JSON):** verdict (likely affects / unclear /
  probably not), the backend component most affected, what changes, why it matters for
  this app, what to check first, and a supporting quote.
- **Grounded or discarded.** The quote must appear word for word in the evidence (case,
  punctuation and line breaks aside; at least 6 words, one unbroken excerpt) and the
  component must exist in the map. Otherwise the brief is dropped and the rules review
  stands unchanged. A brief never changes relevance or closes a review; it only reorders
  (likely first, probably-not last) and explains.
- **No tools, no actions.** One `Converse` call to Amazon Nova Lite, temperature 0, max
  450 output tokens. The model cannot fetch, write or call anything (invariant 5).
- **Hard caps.** A global monthly allowance shared by all workspaces (300 briefs and
  USD 0.50 by default), reserved atomically before each call and settled with the actual
  token cost. The owner's manual pause stops briefs too. Errors skip the brief; they never
  abort a scan. Each review is briefed once (cached on the review), so repeat scans cost
  nothing.
- **Least privilege.** `infra/deploy_free.py --ai-briefs` lets only the scan worker's role
  invoke only `amazon.nova-lite-v1:0`; Bedrock stays denied for every other action, model
  and role. A routine deploy keeps the current setting; `--no-ai-briefs` turns it off.
- Where it shows: a labelled "AI" line on review cards, a full brief first in the review
  dialog (with its quote, model and date, and a note when it reads a different component
  than the rules match), the agent handoff and the digest. All copy that said "no AI"
  now states exactly what is sent when briefs are on.
- The demo's two open reviews carry real Nova Lite briefs generated on 20 October from
  their saved evidence (total cost USD 0.0002).

Measured: about 800–1,600 input and 140–200 output tokens per brief, roughly
USD 0.0001 each.

## Usability

- Overview leads with what needs attention: counts, then the review queue (with AI briefs
  and dates), then "By when", then the source map. Monitoring health folds to one status
  line and opens itself when something needs attention.
- The review dialog leads with the answer (the AI brief), then where it lands, then the
  evidence, then the optional agent handoff.
- Review cards fill the row instead of leaving an empty third column.
